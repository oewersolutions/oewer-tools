How EMI is calculated: the formula, step by step

3 Nov 20266 min readBy the OEWER Tools team

An EMI is the fixed amount you pay a lender every month until a loan is repaid. It looks simple, but it hides a calculation worth understanding, because it tells you how much of each payment is interest and how much reduces what you owe.

The formula

For a reducing-balance loan the monthly instalment is:

EMI = P × i × (1 + i)n ÷ ((1 + i)n − 1)
  • P is the amount you borrow.
  • i is the monthly interest rate: the yearly rate divided by 12 and by 100.
  • n is the number of monthly payments.

A worked example

Borrow ₹10,00,000 at 9% a year for 5 years. The monthly rate is 0.75% and there are 60 payments, which gives an EMI of about ₹20,758. Over five years you pay roughly ₹2,45,500 in interest.

Tip. A lower rate matters less than a shorter tenure when you want to pay less interest in total, but a shorter tenure raises the monthly payment.

Common mistakes

Forgetting to convert a yearly rate to a monthly one is the classic. Another is comparing loans by the monthly payment alone and ignoring the total cost.
